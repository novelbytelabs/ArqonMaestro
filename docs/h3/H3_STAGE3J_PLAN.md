## 1. Stage mission

Stage `3J` implements the workflow-creation judgment layer of Maestro.

It takes the governed workflow-memory substrate earned in `3I` and turns it into a lawful system that can:

* discover repeated governed workflow-like behavior
* infer reusable workflow structure
* score workflow candidates
* evaluate workflow creation risk
* apply situation-shaped rubrics
* choose bounded creation promotion states
* create workflow draft artifacts
* expose durable API-ready draft/library surfaces for later UI/UX

Stage `3J` does **not** implement workflow execution.

Creation remains constitutionally separate from execution.

---

## 2. Stage thesis

The thesis of Stage `3J` is:

**Maestro should not merely notice repeated behavior; it should become increasingly worthy of turning that behavior into reusable workflow artifacts with discipline, taste, calm timing, and earned autonomy.**

That means `3J` is not a generic suggestion feature.
It is the capstone system that proves Maestro can exercise workflow-creation judgment.

---

## 3. Governing doctrine

All `3J` implementation must preserve the following laws:

* creation is separate from execution
* repeated patterns are evidence, not authority
* discovery is not promotion
* promotion is not execution
* risk is first-class
* trust is earned
* one promotion engine, many rubrics
* silence is valid
* low-risk drafts may be auto-created only where policy and trust justify it
* auto-save is stricter than auto-create
* no H23/H24 bypass
* no Stage 3A drift
* no persistence or distributed cache unless explicitly opened
* no hidden workflow artifacts that later matter operationally without inspectability

These are hard constraints.

## 13. Known stage risk zones

These risk zones should remain explicitly tracked throughout implementation:

### Discovery inflation

Too many weak patterns become candidates.

### Abstraction overreach

Variable/optional/slot inference becomes too aggressive.

### Promotion overconfidence

Candidates get promoted too far too early.

### Timing failure

Valid candidates surface in the wrong channel or too often.

### Trust miscalibration

The system acts as though trust has been earned when it has not.

### Draft clutter

Low-value or near-duplicate drafts accumulate.

### Artifact weakness

Drafts are structurally valid but not truly usable, editable, or well organized.

These are the places where `3J` can fail even while looking technically functional.

## 1. Stage thesis

`3J` is the workflow-creation judgment layer of Maestro.

It takes the memory and continuity substrate from `3I` and turns it into:

* workflow candidate discovery
* workflow abstraction
* workflow scoring
* workflow creation risk evaluation
* situation-shaped rubric judgment
* bounded promotion decisions
* trust-calibrated draft creation
* durable draft/library-ready artifact interfaces

`3J` is not execution.

`3J` is the capstone layer where the system begins to prove that it can:

* notice repeated governed behavior
* understand reusable structure
* decide what is worth turning into a workflow artifact
* present or create those artifacts with taste, restraint, and increasing trustworthiness

---

## 2. Constitutional summary of 3J

The whole stage is governed by these core laws:

* creation is separate from execution
* discovery is not promotion
* promotion is not execution
* repeated patterns are evidence, not authority
* risk is first-class
* trust is earned
* silence is valid
* suggestions should be rare enough to preserve trust, but not so strict that elite candidates are buried
* user sovereignty remains intact
* low-risk auto-created drafts may be allowed when explicitly enabled and truly earned
* no H23/H24 bypass
* no Stage 3A drift
* no silent authority accumulation
* no hidden workflow artifacts that later become operationally important without inspectability

## 5. Integrated stage logic

As one integrated system, `3J` works like this:

### Step 1 — discovery

Repeated governed subsequences are found.
Governed reality from `3I` is compressed into candidate-worthy repeated patterns.

**Spec:** `07`

### Step 2 — skeleton inference

Those repeated patterns are turned into reusable workflow structure:

* fixed steps
* variable steps
* optional steps
* slots
* reusable skeletons

**Spec:** `08`

### Step 3 — candidate construction

The inferred workflow becomes a canonical workflow candidate artifact with:

* provenance
* structure
* abstraction
* explainability
* lifecycle fields

**Spec:** `02`

### Step 4 — scoring

The candidate receives the full seven-score surface.

**Spec:** `03`

### Step 5 — risk evaluation

Creation risk is decomposed and aggregated.

**Spec:** `04`

### Step 6 — rubric evaluation

The candidate is judged through:

* baseline best-practices
* class-specific logic
* user-specific trust/preferences
* timing/context rules

**Spec:** `05`

### Step 7 — promotion

The system determines the highest lawful creation action:

* observe
* hold
* suggest
* auto-create draft
* auto-save draft

**Spec:** `06`

### Step 8 — surfacing behavior

The candidate or draft is routed appropriately:

* silent hold
* inbox
* inline
* digest
* quiet draft creation

**Spec:** `09`

### Step 9 — policy and trust shaping

User preference and earned class-specific trust shape the autonomy envelope.

**Spec:** `10`

### Step 10 — artifact creation and storage

Draft/library-ready artifacts are created and exposed through stable API surfaces.

**Spec:** `11`

### Step 11 — validation and closure

The whole stage is proven against doctrine, quality, clutter control, trust, timing, and artifact integrity.

**Spec:** `12`

## 7. What 3J is not

`3J` is not:

* execution
* automation replay
* hidden chaining
* a popup spam engine
* a universal one-rubric workflow judge
* a raw pattern-miner with no taste
* a draft factory with no clutter discipline
* a UI stage
* a workflow runner

It is a **workflow creation judgment system**.

That distinction must stay visible everywhere in the stage.

## 12. What must not drift across the stage

The following truths must remain stable through every 3J implementation slice:

* creation is separate from execution
* repeated patterns are evidence, not authority
* risk is first-class
* trust is earned, not assumed
* one promotion engine, many rubrics
* silence is valid
* suggestions should be accurate and well-timed
* low-risk drafts may be auto-created only where policy and trust allow
* auto-save is stricter than auto-create
* no silent hidden workflow artifacts
* no UI-led architectural drift
* no premature executable semantics

If any implementation slice violates these, it is not aligned with `3J`.
